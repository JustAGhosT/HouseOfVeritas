# House of Veritas investor video plan

- Date: 2026-08-01
- Status: Production-ready plan; product and market claims remain subject to
  the evidence boundaries in the investor deck
- Baton task: `866cebf7-190b-4d88-86f4-823c41ad9f07`
- Companion deck: [Investor pitch deck](2026-08-01-investor-pitch-deck.md)

## Creative objective

Create a premium, discreet, investor-appropriate introduction to House of
Veritas. The video should establish the operational problem, reveal the product
as a structured governance layer, define the target context, and close on the
compliance-first expansion strategy.

Version one should be a **voiceover slideshow with light motion**, not a talking
avatar. This format is faster to produce, easier to revise, and better matched to
the brand's serious, high-trust positioning.

## Master specification

| Item       | Direction                                                        |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Runtime    | 55–60 seconds; target 58 seconds                                 |
| Format     | 16:9 landscape, 1920×1080                                        |
| Frame rate | 24 or 25 fps                                                     |
| Tone       | Premium, calm, trustworthy, discreet                             |
| Palette    | Soft ivory, deep forest/charcoal, muted gold accents             |
| Motion     | Slow push-ins, restrained parallax, clean panel transitions      |
| Voice      | Measured and credible; approximately 130–140 words per minute    |
| Music      | Subtle cinematic corporate underscore; low drama, no vocal track |
| Captions   | Burned-in optional master plus a clean master and `.srt` file    |
| Avatar     | Excluded from version one                                        |

## Visual consistency prompt

Apply this direction to every generated scene:

> Premium, calm, trustworthy corporate film for a modern estate-governance SaaS
> platform; discreet wealth rather than conspicuous luxury; elegant contemporary
> architecture and interfaces; soft natural light; deep forest, charcoal, ivory,
> and muted gold palette; slow cinematic movement; restrained composition;
> realistic materials; polished investor presentation; 16:9; no visible brand
> names, no readable generated interface text, no people looking into camera.

**Global negative prompt:** flashy advertising, nightclub lighting, excessive
gold, opulent mansion clichés, dramatic lens flares, fast zooms, glitch effects,
cyberpunk UI, smiling presenter, talking avatar, distorted hands, unreadable text,
fake logos, watermark, compliance certification badges.

Generated footage should provide atmosphere and motion. All precise interface
copy and branded text should be added during editing rather than generated into
the video frames.

## Scene plan

### Scene 1 — Opening hook

- **Time:** 00:00–00:06
- **Purpose:** Establish the high-value environment and operational stakes.
- **Visual:** Elegant private-estate exterior at golden hour. Use a slow push-in
  or subtle lateral move with generous negative space for text.
- **On-screen text:** `Estate operations are too important for fragmented systems.`
- **Voiceover:** “Complex estates still run critical operations across paper
  files, spreadsheets, messaging apps, and disconnected tools.”
- **Generation prompt:** Luxury private estate exterior at golden hour, elegant
  contemporary architecture, discreet wealth, calm cinematic movement, soft
  natural light, high-trust environment, restrained premium corporate film,
  uncluttered composition, space for headline overlay, no people facing camera.
- **Transition:** Slow dissolve into close operational details.

### Scene 2 — Fragmentation problem

- **Time:** 00:06–00:12
- **Purpose:** Make the fragmented current state tangible.
- **Visual:** Controlled montage of paper files, spreadsheet rows, message
  threads, compliance documents, and a missed reminder. Keep the desk credible
  and professional rather than chaotic.
- **On-screen text:** `Fragmentation creates risk.`
- **Voiceover:** “That fragmentation creates missed deadlines, document
  expiries, weak visibility, slower incident response, and compliance gaps.”
- **Generation prompt:** Close-up montage of estate administration across paper
  files, spreadsheets, messaging apps, printed compliance records, missed
  reminders, and a refined office desk; subtle operational tension; realistic,
  professional, elegant editing; no legible personal information.
- **Transition:** Brief focus pull from paperwork to a clean digital overlay.

### Scene 3 — Risk becomes visible

- **Time:** 00:12–00:18
- **Purpose:** Translate fragmentation into specific operational consequences.
- **Visual:** Expiry alert, unresolved incident, vehicle record, delayed
  approval, and incomplete audit timeline presented as restrained motion
  graphics.
- **On-screen text:** `Missed expiries. Weak visibility. Audit gaps.`
- **Voiceover:** No new sentence; allow Scene 2's voiceover to complete and give
  the risk terms room to land.
- **Generation prompt:** Sophisticated interface overlays showing an expired
  document alert, unresolved property incident, vehicle record, delayed approval,
  and incomplete audit trail; dark elegant enterprise UI; controlled motion;
  abstract data only; no real personal information or certification claims.
- **Transition:** Risk cards align and transform into structured product modules.

### Scene 4 — Solution reveal

- **Time:** 00:18–00:25
- **Purpose:** Introduce House of Veritas as the organizing layer.
- **Visual:** Reveal the investor deck's product-workflow mockup or a current
  product screenshot inside a polished browser frame. If using the mockup, retain
  the label `Illustrative workflow`.
- **On-screen text:** `One secure operating layer.`
- **Voiceover:** “House of Veritas brings estate records, operations, and
  governance into one secure, structured platform.”
- **Generation prompt:** Smooth reveal of an elegant estate-administration SaaS
  dashboard, modular records, operations, and governance sections, refined dark
  green and ivory interface, role-based workflow cues, calm cinematic zoom,
  premium enterprise product presentation, no readable generated text.
- **Transition:** Camera settles into the interface; modules animate in sequence.

### Scene 5 — Platform coverage

- **Time:** 00:25–00:32
- **Purpose:** Demonstrate breadth without turning the film into a feature list.
- **Visual:** Three grouped panels: `Records`, `Operations`, and `Governance`.
  Within them, animate simple icons for documents, staff, tasks, incidents,
  assets, vehicles, expenses, signatures, and audit history.
- **On-screen text:** `Records. Operations. Governance.`
- **Voiceover:** “Manage documents, approvals, staff administration, tasks,
  incidents, assets, vehicles, expenses, e-signatures, and audit trails in one
  place.”
- **Generation prompt:** Elegant motion-graphics sequence of modular enterprise
  interface panels for documents, staff administration, tasks, incidents,
  assets, vehicles, expenses, e-signatures, and audit trails; clean iconography;
  minimal high-trust product design; slow, precise transitions.
- **Transition:** Interface scales down into desktop and mobile devices.

### Scene 6 — Customer context

- **Time:** 00:32–00:39
- **Purpose:** Show who operates and buys the product.
- **Visual:** Estate manager reviewing a desktop workflow, then a brief owner
  dashboard glance on mobile. Avoid surveillance imagery or staff being filmed
  without agency.
- **On-screen text:** `Built for complex households and property employers.`
- **Voiceover:** “It is built for private estates, complex households, and
  property employers where recurring compliance and operational complexity
  create real risk.”
- **Generation prompt:** Professional estate manager using a desktop dashboard
  and mobile device in a refined private-estate office; calm productive workflow;
  respectful human portrayal; realistic operational setting; no exaggerated
  emotion; no person looking into camera; premium business realism.
- **Transition:** Match cut from an open alert to its resolved state.

### Scene 7 — Outcome and value

- **Time:** 00:39–00:46
- **Purpose:** Make the intended value legible and measurable.
- **Visual:** Named document owner, visible expiry status, approval completion,
  incident logged, and audit timeline progressing to resolution.
- **On-screen text:** `Clarity. Accountability. Audit readiness.`
- **Voiceover:** “The goal is greater operational clarity, stronger
  accountability, and audit-ready governance across the estate.”
- **Generation prompt:** Refined dashboard animation with alerts resolving,
  approval completed, audit timeline advancing, structured task completion, and
  compliance status moving to on track; elegant enterprise UI; controlled,
  confident motion; no compliance certification badge.
- **Transition:** Completed workflow expands into the wider platform view.

### Scene 8 — Strategic close

- **Time:** 00:46–00:54
- **Purpose:** Land the investment sequence.
- **Visual:** Wide product overview with three-stage progression. Use the final
  investment-story band from the deck as the visual anchor.
- **On-screen text:** `Start with compliance. Expand into the estate operating system.`
- **Voiceover:** “House of Veritas starts with compliance pain, proves value
  through digitized workflows, and expands into the operating system for estate
  administration.”
- **Generation prompt:** Wide overview of a governance-first software platform,
  subtle motion across a refined interface, premium dark background, three-stage
  progression from compliance to workflow proof to estate operations, clean
  investor-quality composition, calm confidence.
- **Transition:** Fade interface into the brand end card while music resolves.

### Scene 9 — End card

- **Time:** 00:54–00:58
- **Purpose:** Create a clean, memorable brand finish.
- **Visual:** House of Veritas wordmark on a deep forest or charcoal background
  with one muted-gold line. Add the verified website only when the final public
  destination is confirmed.
- **On-screen text:**

  ```text
  House of Veritas
  Secure estate management and digital governance
  ```

- **Voiceover:** “House of Veritas. Secure estate management and digital
  governance for modern estate operations.”
- **Generation prompt:** Minimal premium end screen, deep forest-charcoal
  background, subtle muted-gold accent line, refined logo placement, clean
  typography area, modern discreet enterprise-software branding, no invented
  URL, no generated text.
- **Audio:** Hold the final music note for half a second after the voice ends.

## Edit and motion direction

- Keep cuts between 2 and 4 seconds; avoid rapid montage editing.
- Use slow scale changes of approximately 2–4% for still imagery.
- Animate headlines with short fades or 8–12-pixel vertical movement.
- Keep on-screen headlines visible for at least 2.5 seconds.
- Use one transition family throughout: dissolve, gentle wipe, or panel align.
- Keep lower-thirds and captions inside broadcast-safe margins.
- Duck music approximately 10–14 dB beneath the voiceover.
- Use subtle UI sound design only for one or two key resolution moments.

## Claims and privacy guardrails

- Do not describe House of Veritas as certified, legally compliant, or proven to
  reduce risk unless the relevant evidence exists.
- Prefer `audit-ready`, `designed to`, `the goal is`, and `helps` over guaranteed
  outcomes.
- Keep the workflow mockup labelled as illustrative until it is replaced by a
  verified current product capture.
- Do not present the pilot targets as achieved metrics.
- Use fictional or sanitized information in every interface.
- Do not show identifiable household staff, residents, addresses, documents,
  vehicle registrations, or credentials without explicit rights and consent.
- Confirm commercial usage rights for generated footage, music, fonts, voice,
  and stock assets before publication.

## Runway profile

### Best use in this film

Use Runway as the primary image-to-video option for Scenes 1, 4, and 6. Start
from an approved 16:9 still so composition, palette, and architecture are locked
before motion generation.

Runway's current image-to-video guidance says the image establishes composition,
subject, lighting, and style; the text prompt should focus mainly on motion. It
also recommends direct, positive phrasing and simple iteration. Start with one
motion instruction, then add one refinement at a time.

### Runway workflow

1. Generate or design the first frame outside the video model.
2. Select the current image-to-video model available in the Runway workspace.
3. Upload the clean 16:9 start image.
4. Prompt only the camera, subject, environmental motion, and timing.
5. Generate the shortest useful duration first.
6. Compare at least three variants before adding complexity.
7. If a shot cuts unexpectedly, simplify the prompt or select a longer duration.
8. Export the chosen clean plate and add typography in the editor.

### Runway prompt adaptations

**Scene 1 — estate exterior**

> The camera performs a very slow, smooth dolly forward. Tree leaves move gently
> in a light breeze and warm interior lights remain steady. Continuous seamless
> shot. Restrained cinematic motion.

**Scene 4 — product reveal from the approved mockup**

> The locked camera slowly pushes toward the central workflow panel. A soft
> highlight moves once across the interface surfaces. Minimal motion only.
> Continuous seamless shot.

**Scene 6 — estate manager**

> The locked camera remains nearly still as the manager reviews the desktop,
> makes one deliberate mouse movement, and then glances briefly at the mobile
> device. Natural restrained movement. Continuous professional corporate shot.

Do not paste the global negative prompt into Runway. Its official guidance says
to use positive phrasing; express `no camera movement` as `locked camera`, for
example.

## Pika profile

### Best use in this film

Use Pika for quick atmospheric candidates for Scenes 1 and 2, or a short office
plate for Scene 6. Pika's public product pages currently list Pika 2.5,

1. Select Pika 2.5 image-to-video for brand consistency; use text-to-video only

4. Generate three or four variants using one motion idea per prompt.
5. Promote only the selected take to the required delivery resolution.
6. Use Pikaframes if both the start and end composition must be prescribed.
7. Add every headline, dashboard label, alert, and logo in the editor.

## Cross-tool consistency and iteration

1. Approve still frames before spending credits on motion.
2. Use the same source still across Pika, Runway, and Luma when comparing tools.
3. Change one variable at a time: camera, subject motion, duration, or model.
4. Create three preview candidates per generated scene, not one candidate across
   every tool.
5. Lock a selected take before upscaling or generating extensions.
6. Avoid extending clips unless the edit genuinely needs more than the original
   generation; extensions can introduce drift.
7. Match clips in the editor with one color grade, consistent grain, and a common
   motion cadence.
8. Record tool, model, plan, prompt, seed where supported, settings, source asset,
   generation date, credits, output ID, and selection decision.

### Cost-controlled comparison pass

Run this small test before committing to one generator:

- Generate Scene 1 in Runway, Pika, and one of Firefly, Luma, or Veo.
- Use the same approved first frame and a five-second target.
- Produce no more than three variants per tool.
- Score each blind on composition stability, motion restraint, brand fit,
  artifacts, editability, rights/provenance, and effective cost per usable take.
- Select one primary generator and one fallback for the remaining footage.

Do not choose the winner on prompt price alone. The useful measure is cost per
approved, editable shot.

2. Export the five reusable deck elements at 1920×1080 or higher.

7. Review factual claims, privacy, brand consistency, and mobile readability.

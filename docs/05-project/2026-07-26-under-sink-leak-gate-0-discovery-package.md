# Under-sink leak Gate 0 discovery package

- Date: 2026-07-26
- Status: Ready for owner approval and field execution; no field evidence collected
- Baton task: `9bba1180-b6a4-49cb-b1fc-45bdcbb4cd3c`

## Purpose and decision boundary

This package turns the provisional House of Veritas (HOV) positioning and
Household Resolution Graph (HRG) strategy into a repeatable Gate 0 discovery
exercise. It tests whether owner-managed South African staffed households have
an urgent issue-to-resolution coordination problem before HOV builds another
product capability.

The proposed first issue class is minor visible under-sink drain-joint leak
triage. The promised outcome is limited to making the situation safe, gathering
decision-quality evidence, deciding whether use should stop, and preparing a
qualified handoff. It is not diagnosis, repair, plumbing advice, or permission
to perform work.

This package is grounded in:

- [the provisional positioning decision](2026-07-25-noat-positioning-decision.md);
- [the Household Resolution Graph moat](2026-07-25-household-resolution-graph-moat.md).

No interview, household issue, plumbing review, price observation, willingness
to pay, or pilot outcome has yet been recorded. The accompanying
[evidence log](2026-07-26-under-sink-leak-gate-0-evidence-log.md) is deliberately
empty.

2. Confirm South Africa as the first market to test.
3. Confirm that the customer unit is one private staffed household or small
   private estate, not an HOA, sectional-title scheme, rental portfolio, or
   property-management company.
4. Confirm photo-to-resolution coordination as the problem to test first.
5. Nominate an independent, qualified plumbing reviewer and approve the
   eligibility evidence described below.
6. Approve the privacy and safety protocol, including voluntary frontline
   participation and correction/deletion routes.
7. Accept or revise subscription-funded neutral comparison as the commercial

Changing a threshold is permitted only before the first interview, with the
old value, new value, owner, date, and reason recorded. Results must not be used
to move a threshold after collection starts.

## Scope and non-goals

### In scope

- twelve problem interviews: four principals, four household/estate managers,
  and four voluntary frontline staff members/operators;
- the last three real household issues per participant, for 36 incident records;
- observed tools, handoffs, delays, follow-up, evidence of closure, rework,
  buying language, privacy concerns, and device/data constraints;
- a qualified review of the proposed narrow plumbing triage protocol;
- a concept-only, neutral procurement comparison test funded by a proposed
  household subscription;
- a documented Gate 0 go, conditional go, pivot, or no-go decision.

### Out of scope

- product changes or a concierge pilot;
- accepting new household photos or operating records into HOV;
- directing a participant to recreate a leak or perform any plumbing action;
- tools, disassembly, chemical treatment, repair, or material purchase;
- real checkout, affiliate links, paid ranking, supplier scraping, or claims of
  a best price;
- provider ranking or marketplace recruitment;
- employer-led observation, employee performance scoring, or mandatory staff
  participation;
- cross-household learning or model training;
- claims that HOV saves time or money, improves safety, complies with POPIA, or
  has product-market fit.

## Interview operating procedure

### Sequence

1. Conduct consent and safety screening without the employer present for a
   frontline participant.
2. Ask neutral context questions. Do not describe HOV or the proposed wedge.
3. Elicit the three most recent completed or substantially progressed household
   issues, newest first. Do not cherry-pick plumbing incidents.
4. Reconstruct each incident chronologically using the guide below.
5. Code the incident and participant-level problem signal before showing any
   concept.
6. Ask privacy, device, language, correction, and pilot-authority questions.
7. Show the neutral-comparison concept cards in randomized order.
8. Ask commercial questions without asking for payment or commitment.
9. Confirm whether notes may be retained, corrected, or deleted; record no
   contact beyond what was consented.

### Last-three-issues interview guide

Use recall prompts, not leading claims:

1. "What were the last three things in or around the household that needed
   somebody to notice, decide, arrange, fix, or verify?"
2. For each: "Start at the first sign. What happened next?" Build a factual
   timeline before interpreting it.
3. Who first noticed it, who needed to know, and who decided the next action?
4. Which calls, messages, voice notes, paper records, spreadsheets, apps, or
   in-person conversations were used? Ask for a description; do not copy the
   records by default.
5. What information or evidence was missing, repeated, misunderstood, or sent
   to the wrong person?
6. How many follow-ups or handoffs occurred? How many manager minutes can the
   participant reasonably reconstruct? Mark estimates as estimates.
7. Was use of an asset or area stopped? Was there uncertainty about safety or
   whether to call a professional?
8. Was a service provider called? Who scoped the work and what clarification
   did the provider need?
9. Were materials needed? Who identified, compared, approved, collected, or
   paid for them? Was anything wrong, missing, unused, returned, or delayed?
10. What counted as complete? Who verified it, through what evidence, and after
    how long? Did the issue recur or reopen?
11. What was most frustrating or risky? What worked well enough that they would
    not change it?
12. If the same issue happened tomorrow, what would they do differently?

Do not ask whether "photo-to-resolution" sounds useful until all three incident
records and the unaided problem code are locked.

### Participant-level questions

- Who carries the coordination burden and who controls a pilot or subscription
  decision?
- What words does the participant use for the job, records, household, staff,
  and completion? Preserve short de-identified phrases; do not rewrite them as
  product language during coding.
- What would make a work-record tool feel like surveillance or added paperwork?
- Who should see a report, image, instruction, completion record, or correction?
- What is the minimum useful correction, export, and deletion path?
- Is a personal or shared phone used? What are the practical data, storage,
  camera, connectivity, accessibility, literacy, and language limits?
- What evidence would make a four-week bounded pilot acceptable or unacceptable?

## Measurement rubric

Create one incident row per issue and one summary row per participant in the
evidence log. Preserve raw observations separately from analyst interpretation.

### Incident coding

| Field                      | Allowed value or rule                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Incident completeness      | `complete`, `partial`, or `excluded`; exclusion reason required.                                                                                                                                                   |
| Issue class                | Participant's description plus a broad analyst category; do not force it into plumbing.                                                                                                                            |
| Problem revealed unaided   | `yes` only if fragmentation, unclear ownership/status, repeated reconstruction, missing evidence, unsafe uncertainty, or closure/reopen failure appears before concept exposure.                                   |

| Handoffs and follow-ups    | Non-negative count; use `unknown` rather than guessing.                                                                                                                                                            |

## Privacy, dignity, and safety protocol

### Consent and power imbalance

- Participation is opt-in and may stop at any time without explanation.
- Frontline consent is obtained away from the principal/manager. At least part
  of the interview occurs without them present, and no individual response is
  returned to the employer.
- The recruiter states explicitly that participation, refusal, or withdrawal
  will not affect work, pay, hours, references, access to HOV, or household
  relationships.
- Do not offer an incentive through an employer. If compensation is approved,
  make it role-equitable, direct, disclosed, and not contingent on completing
  sensitive questions.
- Obtain separate choices for interview notes, audio recording, artifact review,
  future contact, and pilot contact. No choice may be bundled.

### Data minimization

- Assign participant IDs `P1-P4`, `M1-M4`, and `F1-F4`. Keep contact details in
  a separate, access-limited recruitment record.
- Do not record a precise address, access/security details, full names of staff
  or family, identity numbers, financial credentials, medical details, children,
  or unrelated household routines.
- Prefer participant narration to copies of WhatsApp chats, photos, invoices,
  or staff records. If an artifact is essential, obtain specific consent,
  redact bystanders and identifiers, and log its purpose and deletion date.
- No real household photo is required for Gate 0. Do not upload discovery data
  to the HOV product or use it for model training.
- Quotes must be short, de-identified, and checked for indirect identifiers.

### Retention and rights

- Before recruitment, the owner must name the data controller, approved storage
  location, authorized researchers, retention period, and deletion owner.
- Participants receive a contact path to withdraw, correct, or delete their
  identifiable notes. The response and completion date are logged.
- Delete recordings after verified transcription or at the approved deadline,
  whichever comes first. Keep the minimum coded evidence needed for the Gate 0
  decision.
- Report aggregate role-level findings. Suppress a segment or quote if the
  combination could identify a person or household.
- This protocol is an experiment safeguard, not a claim of organizational POPIA
  compliance. The owner must obtain appropriate legal/privacy review before
  collecting personal information.

### Safety during research

- Never ask a participant to create, restart, touch, loosen, disassemble, smell,
  taste, energize, or repair an issue for the interview.
- Do not ask someone to enter a confined, wet, electrically exposed, unstable,
  contaminated, elevated, or otherwise uncertain area.
- If an active issue is disclosed, stop research questioning. The participant
  should follow their household emergency process and contact the household
  manager or an appropriately qualified provider. Researchers do not triage it.
- Record a safety incident only at the minimum detail needed for response and
  governance. Do not continue an interview to complete a quota.

## Qualified plumbing reviewer brief

The reviewer's job is to define whether the proposed evidence-only triage class
can be separated safely from professional-only work. It is not to endorse HOV,
provide remote diagnosis for a real household, or authorize DIY repair.

### Eligibility and independence

Before review, record:

- name and business contact in the restricted reviewer record;
- South African trade qualification, registration, license, or other credential
  relevant to the jurisdiction and exact work class, with issuer and expiry
  where applicable;
- recent experience with domestic sink waste systems and the relevant local
  materials/standards;
- professional indemnity/public liability position where relevant;
- conflicts with HOV, suppliers, retailers, or referral partners;
- scope, fee, deliverables, reliance limits, and named approver of the final
  protocol.

The HOV owner must validate eligibility against current local requirements. The
reviewer must be independent of product-ranking incentives and able to reject
the proposed issue class.

### Required protocol outputs

The reviewer must return a versioned, signed brief covering:

1. eligible visible trap/drain-joint configurations and unambiguous exclusions;
2. plain-language observation questions that do not imply diagnosis;
3. minimum photo set and measurement method;
4. immediate stop and escalation conditions;
5. allowed containment only, including any PPE or hygiene limits;
6. draft BOM requirement types and prohibited inferences/substitutions;
7. evidence needed for a qualified-provider handoff;
8. completion and recurrence verification windows;
9. ambiguity rules: missing or conflicting evidence must escalate, not guess;
10. incident reporting, version withdrawal, and re-review triggers.

### Candidate evidence set for reviewer approval

No real photos should be collected until the reviewer approves a protocol. The
candidate set is:

- a context view of the full under-sink cabinet/area from floor level;
- a dry-state view of exposed waste pipework and visible joints;
- close views of the apparent drip location from safe, accessible angles;
- a view showing the relationship to electrical outlets, appliances, supply
  hoses, cabinetry damage, stored chemicals, and containment where safely
  possible;
- a scale reference or measurement method approved by the reviewer; never infer
  diameter or thread from an unscaled image;
- manufacturer/part markings when visible without moving or disassembling
  anything;
- participant observation of whether water appeared during ordinary prior sink
  drainage, without asking them to reproduce the leak;
- the time of observation, containment status, and whether fixture use stopped.

The reviewer must specify which dimensions are required, tolerances, permitted
measuring tools, vantage points, and when a measurement is unsafe or unreliable.

### Minimum stop and escalation candidates

The reviewer may add stricter exclusions. At minimum, the protocol must stop for:

- active flooding or water that cannot be contained;
- hidden source, wall/ceiling ingress, supply-line spray, or hot-water leak;
- proximity to energized equipment, outlets, or damaged wiring;
- sewage backup, strong sewer odour, contaminated water, mould, or health concern;
- damaged cabinetry/structure, inaccessible joint, or work at height;
- need for tools, disassembly, chemical treatment, appliance movement, or repair;
- child/pet exposure; or
- uncertainty by the participant, researcher, system, manager, or reviewer.

An escalation output identifies who should be contacted and what bounded
evidence may be shared. It never asserts that delay is safe.

### Draft BOM rules

- The output is a **reviewed draft repair/handoff list**, not a shopping list or
  DIY kit.
- Each line records requirement, quantity/range, evidence source, confidence,
  reviewer status, compatibility constraint, acceptable alternative rule, and
  `professional confirms` status.
- Separate containment consumables from repair parts and tools.
- Do not infer pipe diameter, thread, seal profile, material, pressure/temperature
  rating, chemical compatibility, certification, or exact replacement from one
  image.
- Never silently substitute dimensions, material, fitting system, brand-specific
  compatibility, certification, finish, or warranty conditions.
- Missing evidence produces `unknown - qualified provider to confirm`, not a
  generic product recommendation.
- No supplier, price, affiliate status, or commission can alter technical fit.
- The reviewer defines what counts as a safety- or compatibility-critical
  correction for later Gate 2 measurement.

### Verification window

The reviewer must define separate checkpoints rather than a single instant
"fixed" status. Candidate checkpoints for review are:

- immediate containment/safe handoff acknowledgement;
- provider completion inspection before ordinary use resumes, where applicable;
- first ordinary-use observation by an authorized person;
- a short follow-up such as 24-48 hours; and
- a recurrence check such as 7-14 days.

The reviewer must replace these candidate durations with approved durations or
state why the class cannot be verified reliably. Closure must record verifier,
evidence, protocol version, observation window, and any recurrence/reopen.

## Neutral subscription-funded comparison test

Run this only after the unaided problem interview is coded. It is a concept
test, not procurement.

### Concept cards

Randomize the order of the first three cards for every interview and record the
order. Keep wording and visual weight equivalent.

| Card                                    | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A: workflow subscription                | The household pays a monthly subscription for issue capture, safe escalation, assignments, evidence, and retained history. Material comparison is not included.                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| B: subscription plus neutral comparison | The household pays a monthly subscription for the workflow. For reviewer-confirmed requirements, HOV manually compares at least two authorized local suppliers using timestamped fit, unit/pack, known landed cost, stock confidence, lead time, warranty/returns, and household policy. No supplier pays to rank. The participant checks out directly with the retailer. |
| C: retailer-funded assistant            | The workflow is free or discounted because a retailer funds it. Results may be limited to that retailer's catalog. Any commercial relationship is disclosed.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| D: current approach                     | The household keeps its present tools and searches or calls suppliers itself.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |

Do not label any card "recommended", "best", or "cheapest". Ask:

1. Which would they choose for the next comparable issue, and why?
2. Which do they trust most and least? What creates or removes trust?
3. What information must be visible to assess exact fit and total cost?
4. Would they pay for B independent of any purchase? Ask open willingness first,
   then test pre-approved price bands chosen by the owner.
5. Who pays, approves, and uses it? What would cause cancellation?
6. Does direct retailer checkout feel limiting or reassuring?
7. Would disclosed referral revenue change trust even if ranking were
   contractually independent? This is research only; do not offer it.

### Comparison prototype rules

If the owner supplies a fictional or reviewer-created requirement example:

- include at least two authorized local suppliers or state `insufficient
comparison`;
- use the same requirement and timestamp across cards;
- normalize item price, quantity, pack size, delivery/service fees, known taxes,
  availability, lead time, returns/warranty, and travel assumptions;
- distinguish exact match, reviewer-approved compatible alternative, and unknown;
- show data freshness and uncertainty;
- provide policy views such as lowest verified landed cost, fastest, and lowest
  compatibility risk without one opaque overall rank;
- disclose commercial relationships as `none in this test`;
- provide no link, QR code, cart, checkout, account collection, purchase button,
  affiliate parameter, or real-time retailer interaction.

Record comprehension, preference, trust reason, rejected option, willingness
to trial, price response, and buyer/approver. Do not count polite interest as a
subscription signal.

## Gate 0 decision rules

### Go to Gate 1 recruitment

All must be true:

1. Twelve qualifying interviews and 36 usable last-issue records are completed,
   or exclusions and replacements are documented before analysis.
2. At least 8 of 12 participants show the problem without prompting.
3. At least 6 of 12 show repeated failure or material manager burden.
4. At least 4 of 12 can authorize a bounded pilot.
5. The signal is not confined to one role: at least 2 of 4 participants in each
   role group show the problem.
6. No critical privacy, coercion, or research-safety incident is unresolved.
7. The owner has approved the privacy protocol and a qualified plumbing reviewer
   has approved or explicitly narrowed a versioned triage protocol.

H5 may shape the commercial experiment but is not required to begin a core
workflow Gate 1 pilot. If fewer than 6 prefer subscription-funded neutral
comparison, remove procurement comparison from the near-term pilot rather than
changing the core Gate 0 result.

### Conditional go

Use only when canonical H2 thresholds pass but a readiness dependency is
bounded, owned, and not safety-critical—for example, a language accommodation
or owner-approved retention workflow. Record the owner, deadline, evidence
needed, and prohibition on accepting pilot data until it closes. Missing
qualified safety review, coerced staff participation, or unresolved critical
privacy risk can never receive a conditional go.

### Pivot

Pivot the segment, buyer, workflow, or issue class when:

- at least 5 but fewer than 8 participants reveal the problem;
- the problem is real but economic authority sits consistently outside the
  proposed private-household customer unit;
- managers have burden but frontline staff reject the workflow as surveillance
  or extra administration;
- the coordination job is strong but the plumbing issue class cannot be safely
  or practically bounded; or
- the workflow signal passes but neutral comparison fails, in which case remove
  procurement from the near-term wedge.

A pivot requires a new pre-registered hypothesis and sample; do not relabel the
same evidence as a pass.

### No-go / kill

Stop the current positioning test when:

- fewer than 5 of 12 participants show the problem without prompting;
- existing tools handle the job adequately for the proposed segment;
- recruitment or participation depends on employer pressure;
- a critical privacy or safety incident cannot be contained;
- no qualified reviewer can define a credible narrow triage boundary; or
- the only perceived retention or value comes from data friction, discounts,
  hidden ranking incentives, or promises HOV cannot support.
